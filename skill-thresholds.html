Notes on handing work to coding agents
Working notes, updated as I learn things the hard way.

The threshold problem: capable people stuck at the door

The gap is rarely the job. It is the twenty minutes of setup standing in front of the job.

There is a specific shape of waste I keep noticing. Someone is entirely capable of doing a piece of work — they have the judgment, the product knowledge, the care — and the task still sits unstarted because the first step requires something they have never done. Cloning the repo. Knowing which command starts the thing. Finding where the test account lives.

The work is not hard for them. The doorway is.

Why this is worse than a normal skill gap

A real skill gap distributes: the person is slower throughout, and they get faster with practice. A threshold gap does not distribute at all. It sits entirely at minute zero, it blocks one hundred percent of the task, and no part of the rest of the work teaches you how to cross it.

That asymmetry is what makes it expensive. The whole task queues behind the one person who can cross the doorway unaided — usually the same overloaded engineer — while a perfectly capable person waits beside it. You are not short on capability. You are short on entrances.

What actually fixes it

Three things, in order of how much they help:

  • Write down the doorway, not the work. The exact clone command, the exact start command, where the credential lives. This is the part experts skip because it is beneath mentioning, and it is the only part that is actually blocking.
  • Make the first run verifiable. Give them one command whose output tells them the setup worked. Without it, a newcomer cannot distinguish “I set it up wrong” from “the task is genuinely confusing”, and most people assume the first and quietly give up.
  • Let an agent hold the tooling knowledge. This is the newer option and the one that changes the arithmetic. The threshold is exactly the kind of narrow, well-documented, mechanical knowledge an agent has in abundance, supervised by a human who has the judgment the agent lacks. The pairing covers both halves.

The thing to be honest about

This does not turn everyone into an engineer, and pretending otherwise sets people up to fail publicly. It widens the band of work a given person can take on, which is a smaller and more useful claim. The tasks that open up are the bounded, checkable ones — which happens to be most of a backlog.
